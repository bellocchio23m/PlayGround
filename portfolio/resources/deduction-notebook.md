# Keep a deduction notebook without memorizing every detail

When a mystery presents times, rooms and conflicting accounts, externalize the information. A small notebook can make the reasoning easier to inspect.

Create three columns headed evidence, interpretation and open question. Put a statement in the evidence column only when you can point to its source in the case. Record your explanation in the interpretation column and keep alternatives in the final column.

For timelines, draw one row per person and mark only events supported by a clue. Leave unknown intervals empty. If two claims conflict, identify the precise overlap before choosing which claim to discard.

A false account may establish that a statement is unreliable. Responsibility still needs the evidence required by the case. Revisit assumptions about clocks, travel and the meaning of a room visit before making an accusation.
